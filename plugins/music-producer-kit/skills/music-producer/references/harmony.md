# Harmony: choose an effect, then realize it as notes

Read for harmonization, reharmonization, chord spelling, voicing or modulation. This is not a compulsory pass for a drum, lyric or mix-only edit.
Working concepts below apply to twelve-tone equal-tempered note examples. Genre conventions and emotional associations are not universal laws.

## 1. Establish what is actually fixed

Distinguish a protected melody, protected chord sequence, protected bass line and a protected recording: they permit different changes.
For harmonization, inspect the melody's pitches, durations, accents, rests and phrase endings before choosing chords.
For a voicing-only request, preserve chord identity, timing and the protected bass; change registers, spacing or doublings only.
A chord symbol does not uniquely specify octave, inversion, rhythm, duration, articulation or instrument.

Determine the tonal or modal center from context; the first chord or a key signature alone is not proof.
A static vamp, modal loop, riff, drone or deliberately nonfunctional passage need not behave like a tonal cadence.
Choose harmonic rhythm independently of surface note density. More chord changes can weaken a long melodic statement.

## 2. A usable spelling baseline

For a major key, diatonic triads are I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii-dim. In C: C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, Bdim.
Their diatonic sevenths are Cmaj7, Dm7, Em7, Fmaj7, G7, Am7, Bm7b5.
Natural minor and functional minor practice are not interchangeable: A natural minor includes G, while E7 leading to Am introduces G#.
Do not raise every sixth and seventh in every minor passage merely because a scale diagram does so.

| Symbol | Interval structure above root, in semitones | Example pitch classes |
|---|---|---|
| C | 0, 4, 7 | C E G |
| Cm | 0, 3, 7 | C Eb G |
| Cmaj7 | 0, 4, 7, 11 | C E G B |
| C7 | 0, 4, 7, 10 | C E G Bb |
| Cm7 | 0, 3, 7, 10 | C Eb G Bb |
| Cm7b5 | 0, 3, 6, 10 | C Eb Gb Bb |
| Cdim7 | 0, 3, 6, 9 | C Eb Gb Bbb |
| Csus4 | 0, 5, 7 | C F G |

Cadd9 adds D without requiring a seventh; C9 conventionally includes the dominant seventh Bb. Do not interchange them.
C/E means a C chord with E as the bass, not C divided by E, and not necessarily a new harmonic root.
The interval recipes specify pitch classes, not an instruction to pile every note into one octave.

## 3. Melody fit before extra color

Inspect simultaneous events, especially sustained notes, repeated accented notes and phrase arrivals.
Passing notes, neighbors, suspensions and appoggiaturas explain some non-chord tones, not every possible use of dissonance.
A melody note outside the triad can be an intended extension, a blues inflection, a pedal or a deliberate friction.
Do not replace a protected note solely because it is absent from a chord-scale table.

Example: a sustained F over C-E-G creates an exposed E/F relationship. Options include retaining that tension, delaying E, using a sus texture, changing register, or reharmonizing if authorized.
These alternatives change different things. Moving the user's F to E is not a voicing edit.
Evaluate tension by register, duration, articulation, loudness and surrounding harmony; a fleeting pitch and a long held pitch are not equivalent.

## 4. Voicing and bass movement

Try redistribution before adding new chord symbols. Keep common tones where useful and move other voices economically, without making minimum motion the only objective.
Wide low-register clusters, excessive sustained doubling and a busy upper voice can obscure a melody. Judge the actual sound, not a fixed cutoff frequency.
A separately played bass allows upper voices to omit a root when the harmonic context remains clear; do not omit the bass the user asked to preserve.

## 5. Color options and their costs

| Goal | Candidate | Inspect before using |
|---|---|---|
| Direct an arrival toward Am | E7 before Am | G# against any protected G; rhythmic room for the extra event |
| Smooth a bass descent | C, C/B, Am or another contextual slash voicing | Whether B is intended as a passing bass, and actual combined notes |
| Borrow a darker color in C major | Fm or another parallel-minor color | Ab against sustained A; not an automatic sadness formula |
| Delay an arrival | Gsus4 or G7sus4 before G | Treatment of C and B; a sus need not always resolve |
| Chromatic dominant approach to C | Db7 in place of G7 | Melody compatibility and bass change; shared guide-tone pitch classes do not make every substitution equivalent |
| Hold the foundation while upper harmony moves | Pedal bass | Accumulated dissonance and the point where the pedal releases |
| Change a held chord internally | One moving inner voice | Whether chord identity and a protected line remain intact |

Select an option that solves the request. Do not walk through the table adding every device.
Diatonic repetition, power chords and plain triads remain valid endpoints.

## 6. Extensions: preserve the disagreement, not a false prohibition

9, 11 and 13 correspond to the second, fourth and sixth scale degrees displaced by an octave; an actual voicing may place them differently.
A major chord's natural 11 can conflict with its third; #11 offers another color but can conflict with a protected natural 4 or the fifth in close spacing.
For m7, natural 13 is a Dorian-associated choice; b13 may belong to an Aeolian context. Neither belongs automatically on every minor chord.
For half-diminished chords, Locrian and Locrian-natural-2 give different ninths. The source's blanket ninth-avoidance advice and its cited jazz exception must not be collapsed into one ban.
A dominant's b9, natural 9, #9, #11, natural 13 and b13 are choices, not a set to stack indiscriminately.
An altered-dominant pitch collection and a half-whole diminished collection are not identical. The actual notes, destination and melody matter more than the word 'jazzy'.

In the archived harmony reference, Japanese-pop rules of thumb and jazz treatments sometimes disagree about destination, extensions and avoid notes.
Keep the relevant conditions attached. Treat an avoid note as a contextual handling warning, not a universally illegal pitch.
The detailed comparison tables remain bundled; do not claim all their source-book assertions have been independently verified.

## 7. Tonicization, modulation and transposition are different tasks

An E7-Am gesture inside C does not by itself prove a sustained move to A minor.
A modulation establishes a new center through subsequent context; duration alone is not a complete test.
Transposing an entire song for a singer is not the same task as inserting a mid-song key change.
A pivot chord, common tone, new dominant or direct cut can make a transition intentional. Choose smoothness or surprise according to the request.
For a planned return, design the return seam as well as the outward move. Recheck ranges and fixed audio tuning after any authorized transposition.
Never apply a key change to a protected melody as an incidental fix for a weak chorus.

## 8. Stop and compare

Compare the candidate with the original over enough context to hear the phrase and its destination.
Keep the version that serves the user, including the original when added color does not help.
Without an audio path, report note-level compatibility and remaining listening questions, not that a version sounds better.
