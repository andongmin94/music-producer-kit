# Lyrics and vocals: activate only when relevant

## Content and working direction

Determine what the singer is expressing, to whom, and in what situation when those questions help the task.
Direct emotion, an attitude, a scene, a story or a nonspecific vocal hook can each be valid.
A photographable object, a complete narrative, deliberate non-rhyme or a changed final chorus is never compulsory.
Preserve approved words and melody when the request is only alignment or production.
Music-first: read the actual melody's rhythm, held notes, pickups and rests before fitting text.
Lyrics-first: shape a musical phrase around the text, then recheck the sung alignment after melody changes.
Keep one shared section map rather than separately maintained conflicting tempo/key/section copies.

## Language-specific checks

Use actual intended pronunciation, not character count as a universal syllable count.
English: examine word stress and phrase emphasis against musical accents; counting letters is irrelevant.
Japanese: distinguish mora timing from syllables and notated notes. A mora is not obligatorily one musical beat or one note.
Korean: inspect intended sung syllables, liaison, contractions and breathing. A final consonant does not automatically forbid a held or high note.
A singer may sustain the vowel and close the consonant later; treat difficult articulation as a review question, not an automatic word replacement.
Mandarin/Cantonese: tone-melody alignment is language-specific and context-sensitive. Flag uncertain pronunciation rather than importing unrelated language rules.
Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese and Korean source modules and their reference files are preserved in the [local library](../../../library/README.md). Use the relevant language evidence without importing another language's constraints or the archived workflow. For languages not covered by those sources, state the expertise gap; do not invent verified support.
A syllable may span multiple notes; several syllables may fit a short rhythmic gesture. Do not silently assume one character equals one MIDI event.

## Vocal performance and tools

Distinguish lyric writing, melody guides, real recording edits and singing synthesis.
Harmony and doubles can serve fullness, emphasis or a character contrast; they are not mandatory layers.
Timing of consonant attack and vowel sustain affects phrasing. Do not add pitch errors merely to simulate humanity.
Keep the original vocal recording. Do not infer usable range, voice identity or permissions from a reference artist.
Verify an available transcription/editor/synthesis path before promising audio. Written delivery instructions do not generate a vocal recording.
