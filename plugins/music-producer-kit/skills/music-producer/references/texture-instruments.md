# Texture, instrumentation and counterlines

Read for part writing, layer conflicts, orchestration or a section that needs a different presence. It is not a fixed track-count, EQ or loudness recipe.

## 1. Give each current part a job

A part may carry a lead, bass foundation, harmonic support, rhythmic punctuation, independent countermelody or transition.
Several instruments can perform one job; one instrument can change jobs by section. Do not invent tracks to fill a roster.
Describe the intended foreground in plain language. Equal competing voices can also be intentional, for example in a duet.
A register label is insufficient for execution: use actual note ranges and check the chosen sound.

## 2. Diagnose a conflict before changing the sound

| Observed problem | Candidate change within arrangement | Cost to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Lead loses focus during a busy accompaniment | Reduce simultaneous attacks or answer in gaps | Loss of intended urgency |
| Sustained chords obscure a bass line | Shorten support notes, remove a doubling or spread registers | Thinner texture or altered sustain |
| Two independent hooks compete | Alternate phrases or stagger peaks | Less simultaneous density |
| Added octave layer takes over the lead role | Change its rhythm, register, tone or level | Loss of the intended blend |
| A chorus does not contrast | Change support articulation, range or preceding texture | Whether an approved preceding section would be touched |

Use measured or actually heard evidence. A spectral picture alone does not prove which arrangement the listener will prefer.
If the request protects note data and permits only mixing, stay in the permitted parameters; an arrangement rewrite is a proposal, not an automatic first step.
Do not prescribe a universal high-pass cutoff, midrange cut or sidechain chain.

## 3. Combine or separate deliberately

For one composite voice, try unison, octave doubling or a blended voicing, then inspect attack and release compatibility.
For independent voices, contrast rhythm, articulation, range, timbre or timing. Contrast need not be present on every axis.
Do not assume a brighter or higher part is always the foreground. Context, density and the listener's attention matter.
A bass-and-pad combination can leave more room by changing durations without altering the harmony.
An electronic texture may intentionally blur individual sources; clarity of every acoustic instrument is not a universal goal.

## 4. Counterline versus harmony doubling

A harmony doubling closely follows the primary line; a countermelody develops its own phrase while fitting the combined music.
Either can be useful. Counterpoint is not a badge that every professional arrangement must include.
Consider complementary rhythmic activity, consonance/dissonance handling, motion direction and staggered phrase peaks.
Similar, contrary and oblique motion are options. Parallel fifths or octaves are not forbidden in every popular or synthetic texture.
Strict species exercises and invertible counterpoint have narrower conventions; load their detailed source only when that task is actually requested.

Original contour example: lead C5-D5-E5-G5 over two bars; a support voice can hold G4 while the lead moves, then answer after the lead rests.
The final notes and rhythm depend on the actual harmony. A contour sketch is not ready-to-run MIDI and does not by itself prove a pleasing combination.
Do not apply a rule requiring at least two complementary axes to decide whether music is acceptable.

## 5. Instrument behavior and available tools

For acoustic realism, check playable range, transition speed, articulation and sustained breath/bow behavior for the actual instrument.
Use the local source's instrument-specific references rather than a generic orchestral range guessed from memory.
For sample instruments, verify articulation switching, velocity layers, sustain and expression mapping in the loaded library.
CC7, CC11, CC74, keyswitch notes and program changes do not mean identical things in every instrument.
A General MIDI program is not an Ableton device identifier. A VST parameter API is not proof of access to its internal browser or lyric editor.
For intentionally synthetic music, impossible acoustic gestures may be allowed; document them only when they affect the request or editability.

## 6. Sound selection from a functional brief

Choose attack, sustain/release, range, harmonic/noise character and role before browsing presets or buying another instrument.
Start with existing sounds and licensed local samples. Compare candidates in the intended part, not only in solo preview.
A loop may bring fixed chords, timing and a recognizable phrase. A one-shot provides different editing freedom.
Check sample tuning, actual rhythmic fit and release tails where tools permit. Filename BPM/key tags are clues, not verified measurements.
Purchases, credit use and external uploads remain permission decisions, not sound-design defaults.

## 7. Section development without compulsory escalation

A lift may come from a wider register, changed note lengths, harmonic arrival, altered groove or a contrasting lead articulation. More layers and more level are not mandatory.
A drop in density before the chorus is one option; do not edit the previous section if the user protected it.
Repeated choruses may have identical arrangement, different delivery, or different support. Choose rather than automatically vary.
For stable BGM, a deliberately even profile and consistent width can be functional. Do not add a climax merely to pass a checklist.
